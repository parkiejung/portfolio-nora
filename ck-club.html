Overview

What's with CK Club?

CK Club is a loyalty app by Circle K, a famous convenience store chain in Vietnam. With the appearance of several convenience chains (GS25, Family Mart, Seven-Eleven,.... customer retention is more challenging than ever, the company wants some changes to improve the situation.

Read more about CK Club app
Target customers
Individual customers that visit Circle K regularly. Customers who care about deals and promotions, those who are sentisive to price and want to make the most from their purchases.
App domain
Loyalty, Commercial
Customers use app for:
Track loyalty points, track purchase history, find store locations, find discount deals
Company uses app for
Keep loyal customers, increase impression for partner's campaigns

Hypothesis problem to solve

To learn how a loyalty app work and to avoid redesigning UI that did not based on any real context, I came up with a hypothesis problem to work on.

"By Aug 2023, after a free-drinks giveaway marketing campaign, CK Club doubled the number of new user comparing to the previous month. However after 2 weeks, the number of Active Users reduced by 80%, making a great loss on marketing investment."

As a product designer working on this app, my job was to identify the problem and propose the solution to keep users from leaving app early.

This project focused on solving a hypothesis problem that I assume might happen to a loyalty app. This project is only for the purpose of learning, and was not a job for Circle K.

The problems

High install rate and high churn-rate.

To clearly understand the problem, my job was to find the answer to the research question:

"What makes user come to use app, and what stop them from keep using app?"

Research

Listening before designing.

Three methods ran in parallel to understand where friction actually came from — not just where users said it came from.

User Interviews — 7 participants
Split into current users and churned users. 5–7 min sessions at Circle K stores during evening hours. Covered motivation for downloading, current habits, feature awareness, and expectations.
Field Observation — 22 transactions
Tracked live checkout behavior at a Circle K location. Measured time, payment method, and step-by-step flow for CK Club users vs. non-users.
Heuristic Evaluation
Audited the current app against Nielsen's usability principles: visibility of system status, consistency, recognition over recall, and help/documentation.

Key Findings

Three problems, all pointing at the same gap.

Users weren't disengaged — the app was actively getting in their way.

Finding 01
Checkout is too slow and gives no feedback
5 of 7 users said the app slows them down at checkout, especially on a poor connection. Opening the app alone averaged 7.7 seconds during the queue. Users who paid by e-wallet had to switch between two apps mid-checkout. Critically, the app gives no confirmation that points were added — users had no way to verify the transaction registered.
→ Streamline checkout. Allow points accumulation via phone number as an alternative to QR scanning.
Finding 02
Users don't understand what points are for
The stamp-for-reward program was the only feature that kept users engaged — it had a clear, concrete goal. In contrast, 7 of 7 users did not know what the membership tier system was for or what benefits their tier offered.
"I open the QR to collect stamps and check how many I've earned. When I hit 5, I get a free drink — the cashier handles the redemption automatically."
→ Restructure the home screen to surface membership status, QR code, and next-reward progress immediately on open.
Finding 03
Key features are hidden and underused
100% of interviewed users did not know the app had a store locator or transaction history — and assumed those features didn't exist. The app also lacks an in-app notification tab, so points updates and payment confirmations are never surfaced.
→ Conduct card sorting to redesign the app's information architecture.
Reflection

The retention problem was really a trust problem.

Users weren't disengaged — they were uncertain. They didn't know if the app was working, didn't know what they were earning, and couldn't find the features that might have made it worthwhile.

The drop-off wasn't about the reward program being weak. It was about the app failing to communicate its own value at every touchpoint. Getting the checkout flow and home screen right isn't UX polish — it's the design doing what the product was built to do.

Next: usability testing on both the current app and the redesigned prototype, comparing task completion time, steps, and error rates — with focus on checkout friction and feature discovery.
